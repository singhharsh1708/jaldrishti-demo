What to trust

  • Rain is a synthetic storm, the same everywhere on the site. It is not a radar nowcast.
  • Hydraulic parameters (Manning roughness, inlet size and spacing, catchment width) are assumed values, not calibrated against observed floods.
  • The street surface is 1D street channels along the surveyed drains, not a 2D grid. Streets without a surveyed drain are not modelled.
  • Segment depth is the larger of its two end-junction street depths.

How travel time is set

Speed on a flooded segment follows the depth-disruption curve of Pregnolato et al. (2017): v = 0.0009 w² − 0.5529 w + 86.9448 km/h with water depth w in mm, capped at an assumed km/h for city streets. Segments with cm of water or more are closed: every metre of closed street carries a large time penalty, so the safe route avoids closed streets whenever any open route exists. When none exists, the card says so and shows the least flooded route: its deepest water is as shallow as possible, then it crosses the least length of closed street.

The graph is the segments that carry a surveyed drain, treated as two-way streets. It is not the full road network. Depths are model output under a synthetic storm.

From the browser console: JalDrishti.route(272, 346, {t: 100, storm: 60, state: "clean"})
